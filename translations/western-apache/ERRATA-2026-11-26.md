# ERRATA — Western Apache lane, 2026-11-26

## Surah 112 (Al-Ikhlas) — three seats corrected under Robert Tilford's ruling

**How this arose.** On 2026-11-25 Robert raised the pivot question: this lane, like 213 others on the shelf, was rendered from the Locked English Master rather than from the Arabic, and he directed a test of what that cost. On 2026-11-26 the desk built the test he designed (record: `~/workspace/.tools/pivot-audit/SURAH-112-TEST.md`): Surah 112 rendered fresh from the Arabic into Western Apache from this lane's attested stock, drafted before the shelf manuscript was opened, then laid beside the shelf verse by verse. Seven divergences were named: one VERIFIED difference (112:3, begetting rendered as child-making), four differences of construction, and two gaps held open. No divergence in the four verses traced to the English pivot; the one verified difference traced to the attested stock, which the test's builders believed held no active birth verb and no word for "equal."

The same morning, at Robert's direction to keep looking, the desk ran a precision hunt over the whole held stock (record: `~/workspace/.tools/pivot-audit/SURAH-112-PRECISION-HUNT.md`). It found: at 112:3, an active birth verb the glossary had never listed (the stem -chįį, "to bear, to bring forth"), though no birth verb is anywhere predicated of a father in the corpus, whose uniform father-pattern is born-to; at 112:2, no refuge word (NOT FOUND as a lexeme), but two receipted devices, constructions C5 and C41, from which a labelled construction can carry the besought-by-all sense of Ṣamad; at 112:4, the equality word dáłełt'ee, receipted in this lane's own concordance all along but never entered in the glossary.

**Robert's ruling (2026-11-26, ~07:37 AM CST), in his words:** "I'll go with the Clean win" — the 112:4 dáłełt'ee candidate adopted outright — "We'll go with best recommendation on the rest." The desk's best recommendation, adopted by that word, was to seat all three candidates as the hunt specified. The seatings below were executed the same morning. Verse 1 is untouched. Nothing else in the manuscript changed.

## The three seatings

### 112:2 — the refuge sense of Ṣamad, seated as a clause riding beside the standing wording

- **Before:** *Bik'ehgo'ihi'nań, Dahazhį' Golį́į́híí nlįį.*
- **After:** *Bik'ehgo'ihi'nań, Dahazhį' Golį́į́híí nlįį, nṉee dawa yiba' sidaahíí.*
- **What was added:** *nṉee dawa yiba' sidaahíí* — "the One on whom all rest," a labelled construction on construction C41's receipted parts, seated beside the standing C4 eternity rendering exactly as the hunt directed. The standing wording is unchanged inside the verse.
- **Receipts:** *yiba' sidaa*, "resting on, waiting for" — James 5:7, where the corpus reads "k'edileehíí … yiba' sidaa," the farmer resting his expectation on the fruit of the earth; standing in this lane as construction C41, "trust in God." The nominalizing *-híí* is the corpus's own relative ending, the same ending C4 rides in *Golį́į́híí*. *dawa*, "every, all" — glossary §F (UCLA 1996 #40; NT passim). The hunt's finding stands beside the seating: no single attested word carries Ṣamad's refuge sense; this clause carries it descriptively, under a construction flag, and is not a title.

### 112:3 — "He begets not," reseated on the corpus's genealogy pattern

- **Before:** *Doo hadíń bizhaazhé áyíílaa da, doo bizhaazhé alzaa da.* — "He made no one His child; He was not made a child."
- **After:** *Doo hadíń Bik'ehgo'ihi'nań bá hizhchįį da, doo bizhaazhé alzaa da.* — "No one was born to God; He was not made a child."
- **What changed:** the first clause only. The shelf's child-making clause (*áyíílaa*, "made") is replaced by the born-to construction, which denies exactly what the Arabic denies — offspring by birth, with God in the father's seat — where the old wording denied a child by making as well as by birth, a wider denial than the verse speaks. The second clause (*doo bizhaazhé alzaa da*, on *alzaa*, NT Matt 3:4) stands byte-identical; the pivot test graded it a difference of construction only, and no candidate was offered for it.
- **Receipts:** *hizhchįį*, "was born to" — NT corpus, six occurrences, first at Matt 1:2, "Isaac Abraham bá hizhchįį," Isaac was born to Abraham; its repeated form *náánázhchį́* stands twenty-nine times through the genealogy. *bá*, "to him, for him," the genealogy's father-marker, in the same quoted verse. *hadíń*, "anyone, no one" — NT corpus, 464 occurrences, first at Matt 1:19. The negation frame *doo … da* is the corpus's own (glossary §J). The hunt's limit rides with the seating: the active birth verb -chįį ("to bear, to bring forth"; *yishchįį*, nine occurrences, first at Matt 1:3, of Tamar; also Matt 1:16, Luke 2:7, Rev 12:5; future *yiłchii* at Luke 1:13; predicated of abstractions at James 1:15) is predicated of mothers and abstractions only, never of a father, and was therefore NOT seated with God as its subject; the born-to construction is the most precise rendering the stock receipts.

### 112:4 — kufuʾ, "an equal," seated with the recovered equality word

- **Before:** *Doo hadíń bik'ehgo at'éé da.* — "No one is like Him."
- **After:** *Doo hadíń Bik'ehgo'ihi'nań bił dáłełt'ee da.* — "No one is equal with God."
- **What changed:** the whole verse. Likeness (*bik'ehgo at'éé*) is replaced by equality (*dáłełt'ee*): a thing may be like God in a respect without being His equal, and the Arabic denies the stronger thing. This is the seat Robert named "the clean win."
- **Receipts:** *dáłełt'ee* — Western Apache NT corpus, 14 occurrences, first at Matt 11:17 (CONCORDANCE-APW.md, s.v.); its equality sense fixed by Matt 20:12 ("you have made them equal to us" in wages) and 2 Peter 1:1 (the Greek isotimos, "equal in value," rendered "bił dáłełt'ee"); the word already stood in this manuscript at eight seats (2:6, 2:70, 2:118, 2:165, 3:64, 3:113, 3:162, 4:42) but had never entered the glossary, which is why both builds in the pivot test missed it. *bił*, "with," stands inside both quoted receipts. *hadíń* as at 112:3 above. A glossary entry for dáłełt'ee was added the same day (GLOSSARY-APW.md, Section J115), carrying the hunt's honesty note: where the corpus renders "equal with God" as a phrase (John 5:18; Phil 2:6) it uses oneness, *yił dała' nlįį*, a stronger claim than the Arabic makes; on the Arabic's own wording, dáłełt'ee is the more precise road.
