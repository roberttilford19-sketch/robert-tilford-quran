Friends, the Western Apache Quran is on the shelf.

All 6,236 verses of the Quran, rendered into Western Apache — Ndee bizaad — from my English Ayah Companion translation. 6,233 verses stand fully rendered. I am going to be plain with you about the other three, because plain is the only way I know how to do this: at 2:61 we could not find a Western Apache word for garlic or for lentils in any published source anywhere, and at 52:29 and 69:42 we could not find a word for a soothsayer. Those seats are marked right in the text, in the open, where anybody can see them. We did not invent words and call them Apache. That is not how this work is done.

Every other word in this book comes out of the published record — the Western Apache New Testament and the published sources named in the glossary that travels with the book, so any checker can walk any word back to where it was found. Where a rendering rests on a construction instead of an attested phrase, the glossary says so. At one seat, 2:69, there is a note right in the text asking for help: if you can correct it, you will be named a co-contributor of this work, with thanks.

This translation was prepared with the help of artificial intelligence, under my direction. I am a translator in Hays, Kansas, not a speaker of Western Apache, and the book says that about itself. It is not for sale. It is a gift — free to read, free to download, free to pass on. That is the whole point of the Ayah Companion: the most people having the most access.
