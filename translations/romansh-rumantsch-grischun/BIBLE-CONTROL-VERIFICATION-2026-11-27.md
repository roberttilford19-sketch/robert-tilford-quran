# Bible-Control Licence Verification — Romansh (Rumantsch Grischun) Quran Lane

**Date of verification pass:** 27 November 2026 (desk time; sources fetched 27–28 November 2026 UTC).
**Scope:** Research and record only. Nothing was translated, no one was contacted, nothing was copied from any Bible text. Per house law, Bible material is used as fact and citation only, whatever its licence; this record settles the licence position title by title, as the lane brief (§4 of `~/workspace/goals/endangered-language-quran-translations/hidden_files/CANDIDATE-BRIEF-ROMANSH.md`) requires before any reliance.
**Method and tiers:** "VERIFIED" means the desk found the evidence itself on a real page (publisher, library catalogue, platform or scan-platform page) and cites the URL with the operative wording quoted or closely paraphrased. "CANDIDATE / NOT VERIFIED" means exactly that — the point is recorded as unproven, with what was found instead. No licence is guessed anywhere in this record.
**A note on public-domain-by-age reasoning:** Switzerland and the EU apply a copyright term of life of the author plus 70 years. Where this record calls a historical text public domain by age, it names the publication date and, where a source gives them, the translators' death dates on which that reasoning rests. The status of a *scan* is stated separately from the status of the *underlying text* in every section, because the two are different questions.

## 1. Jachiam Bifrun — New Testament in Putèr (Upper Engadine), 1560

**(a) Bibliographic identity.** *L'g Nuof Sainc Testamaint da nos Signer Jesu Christi*, translated by Jachiam (Giachem) Bifrun of Samedan, printed at Poschiavo in 1560; New Testament only, in the Putèr idiom (Upper Engadine Romansh). It is the first Romansh Bible translation. Sources: the Swiss-literature account states that the first book printed in Romansh (Poschiavo, 1552) was Bifrun's catechism and "the next a translation of the New Testament: *L'g Nuof Sainc Testamaint da nos Signer Jesu Christ*, also at Poschiavo, but in 1560, both works by Jachiam Bifrun/Giachem Bifrun" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literature_of_Switzerland ; Lexilogos lists "*L'g Nuof Sainc Testamaint* da nos Signer Jesu Christi (1560)" by Bifrun and calls the work "the first translation of the Bible into Romansh" — https://www.lexilogos.com/english/romansh_dictionary.htm . A transcribed text of the 1560 edition also stands on Italian Wikisource — https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/L%E2%80%99g_Nouf_Sainc_Testamaint .

**(b) Digital text availability.** VERIFIED: a digitised copy stands on e-rara (the Swiss rare-books platform), held by the Kantonsbibliothek Graubünden, under the persistent identifier DOI 10.3931/e-rara-19331 — https://www.e-rara.ch/kbg/doi/10.3931/e-rara-19331 (the page was fetched; its fetched text confirms the title and holding library, though the licence field of that specific record was not rendered in the fetched text — see (c)). Lexilogos links both the 1560 edition and the 1607 reprint to e-rara versions — https://www.lexilogos.com/english/romansh_dictionary.htm . A scholarly reprint edition (Theodor Gartner, 1913, *Das Neue Testament: erste rätoromanische Übersetzung von Jacob Bifrun, 1560*) is listed with page images at HathiTrust (US access only) by the Online Books Page — https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/book/browse?type=lcsubc&key=Raeto-Romance+language+--+Periodicals&c=x .

**(c) Copyright / licence status.** Underlying text: **public domain by age — VERIFIED as to the facts the reasoning rests on.** The work was published in 1560, more than 460 years ago; no life-plus-70 term can subsist in a 1560 publication. Scan terms: e-rara's general position, as stated on its own pages, is that "this digital copy can be downloaded free of charge; the type of licensing and the terms of use are indicated in the title information for each document individually" — https://www.e-rara.ch/download/pdf/2170337.pdf (platform wording carried in that document's terms notice). On other Zentralbibliothek Zürich titles on e-rara, the per-title licence field reads "Licence: Public Domain Mark" with usage wording "Available for free download; in the event of reproduction or exploitation … the holding institution asks you … to provide indication of sources including details of the author, location and persistent identifier" — e.g. https://www.e-rara.ch/zuz/content/titleinfo/14231134 . The Bifrun record's own licence field was **not** rendered in the fetched page text, so the per-title scan licence for this specific copy is recorded as **CANDIDATE / NOT VERIFIED** (platform pattern: Public Domain Mark), while the underlying text's public-domain status is not in doubt.

**(d) House-use position.** Public domain text (1560); e-rara scan freely downloadable, per-title licence field not directly verified — usable as citation/control; confirm the title-info licence field before any bulk reuse of the scan.

## 2. Luci Gabriel — New Testament in Sursilvan, 1648

**(a) Bibliographic identity.** New Testament in the Sursilvan idiom, published 1648 by Luci Gabriel, son of Steffan Gabriel. Sources: "The first translation of the New Testament into Sursilvan was published in 1648 by the son of Steffan Gabriel, Luci Gabriel" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romansh_language ; the same statement in the Bible-translations survey — http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible_translations_into_Romansh . Luci Gabriel's dates are given as 1597–1663 in the e-rara contributor listing for the 1718/1719 Bible (see §4) — https://www.e-rara.ch/search/quick?&query=tartar&facets=alpha.title1%3D%22s%22%20and%20language%3D%22roh%22 .

**(b) Digital text availability.** **NOT FOUND in this pass.** Searches for a digitised 1648 Gabriel New Testament on e-rara, Google Books and the Internet Archive returned no copy of the 1648 edition. What the searches did return is a *later* edition of Gabriel's translation: *Il Niev Testament da Niess Segner Jesus Christus: vertius en Romonsch da la ligia Grischa tras Lucius Gabriel*, printed at the order and cost of the British and Foreign Bible Society in 1870 (Oxford University copy, digitised 3 July 2007, "Full view") — https://books.google.com/books?id=sAMJAAAAQAAJ . That 1870 printing is part of the 1870 Bible treated in §5 and is not evidence of a digital 1648 text.

**(c) Copyright / licence status.** Underlying text: **public domain by age — the reasoning rests on** publication in 1648 and the translator's death in 1663 (source for the dates: the e-rara contributor listing cited in (a)); a life-plus-70 term expired in the 18th century. Scan status: not applicable — no scan was found, so there are no scan terms to record. The licence position of the *text* is therefore settled, but there is at present **no verified digital copy to rely on**.

**(d) House-use position.** Public domain text (1648; translator d. 1663); no digital text found — not usable as a control until a digitised copy is located and its scan terms checked.

## 3. Complete Bible in Vallader — Jacob Dorta and Jachen Anton Vulpi, Scuol, completed 1674 / printed 1679

**(a) Bibliographic identity.** *La Sacra Bibla*, the complete Bible in the Vallader idiom (Lower Engadine), translated by Jacob Dorta (ca 1630–ca 1697) with his brother-in-law Jachen Anton Vulpi (1629–1706), printed at Scuol; translation completed in 1674, first copies off the press in 1679. Source: King's College London's exhibition account — "In 1664 Dorta … joined forces with his brother-in-law Jachen Anton Vulpi (1629-1706) to produce a complete translation of the Bible into the local Vallader dialect … Relying heavily on … Giovanni Diodati … in Geneva in 1607, they laboured for ten years over their translation. It was finally completed in 1674 and approved by the synod of Chur. Five years later the first copy came off the press" — https://kingscollections.org/exhibitions/specialcollections/bible/european-bible/swiss-romansch-bible . The Willard Fiske Rhaeto-Romanic catalogue (Cornell, 1894) lists the edition as "La sacra Bibla; vertida in lingua rumanscha d'Ingadinna Bassa, traes J. A. Vulpio et J. Dorta … Scuol, 1678-79", calling it the "First edition of the Bible in the Lower Engadine dialect, and the first book printed at Scuol" — https://archive.org/stream/catalogueofrhaet00corn/catalogueofrhaet00corn_djvu.txt .

**(b) Digital text availability.** **NOT VERIFIED / NOT FOUND in this pass.** No digitised copy of the 1679 Scuol Bible was found on e-rara, HathiTrust, Google Books or the Internet Archive in the searches run. The King's College London page is an exhibition description of a physical copy, not a digital text — https://kingscollections.org/exhibitions/specialcollections/bible/european-bible/swiss-romansch-bible . This is recorded plainly as a gap: the edition exists and is well documented, but this pass did not verify a freely readable or machine-usable digital text of it.

**(c) Copyright / licence status.** Underlying text: **public domain by age — the reasoning rests on** publication in 1679 and the translators' death dates as given by the King's College London account (Dorta ca 1630–ca 1697; Vulpi 1629–1706); any life-plus-70 term expired by the late 18th century at the latest. Scan terms: none to record, since no scan was verified.

**(d) House-use position.** Public domain text (1679; translators d. ca 1697 / 1706); digital text NOT VERIFIED — cannot yet serve as the lane's digital control despite being the earliest complete Romansh Bible.

## 4. Whole Bible in Sursilvan — "Bibla da Cuera", 1717–1719

**(a) Bibliographic identity.** *La S. Bibla: quei ei, tut la soinchia scartira, ner tuts ils cudischs d'ilg Veder a Nief Testament: cun ils cudischs apocryphs: messa giu ent ilg languaig Rumonsch da la Ligia Grischa* — the complete Bible, with the Apocrypha, in the Romansh of the Grey League (the Sursilvan/Oberland written variety), printed at Chur (Coira/Cuera) by Andrea Pfeffer, dated 1718 [i.e. 1719], the edition conventionally cited as published 1717–1719 and known as the *Bibla da Cuera*. Sources: the e-rara catalogue record gives the full title, the imprint "A squitschada en Coira: tras Andrea Pfeffer …, ent ilg on da Christi 1718 [i.e. 1719]", and the holding copy "ZB Zürich, Bibl 121" — https://www.e-rara.ch/search/quick?facets=domainGroup%3D%22zuz%22+and+language%3D%22roh%22+and+alpha.title1%3D%22s%22&query=Ponet ; the same record names contributors Peter Saluz [1659–1729], Martin Nicolaus Anosi [1678–1743], Christian Caminada [1664–1730], Steffan Gabriel [1565–1638] and Lucius Gabriel [1597–1663] — https://www.e-rara.ch/search/quick?&query=tartar&facets=alpha.title1%3D%22s%22%20and%20language%3D%22roh%22 . The 1717–1719 publication span and "first complete translation of the Bible" description: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romansh_language .

**(b) Digital text availability.** VERIFIED: the complete work stands digitised on e-rara from the Zentralbibliothek Zürich copy (shelfmark Bibl 121), with page images **and searchable OCR page text** — the fetched OCR text of the opening and of interior pages (e.g. Genesis/Exodus) is live at https://www.e-rara.ch/zuz/content/pagetext/9246688 and https://www.e-rara.ch/zuz/content/pagetext/9246757 . The OCR text layer makes this a machine-usable digital text, not merely page images.

**(c) Copyright / licence status.** Underlying text: **public domain by age — VERIFIED as to the facts the reasoning rests on.** Publication 1718/1719 (imprint above); every named translator/contributor died by 1743 at the latest (death dates in the e-rara contributor listing cited in (a): 1638, 1663, 1729, 1730, 1743). A life-plus-70 term expired more than two centuries ago. Scan terms: e-rara states of its documents generally that a digital copy "can be downloaded free of charge" with the licensing type "indicated in the title information for each document individually" — https://www.e-rara.ch/download/pdf/2170337.pdf . The platform's standard per-title licence field on Zentralbibliothek Zürich holdings reads "Licence: Public Domain Mark — Usage: Available for free download; in the event of reproduction or exploitation of the document or parts thereof, the holding institution asks you, in accordance with usual scientific practices, to provide information, indication of sources including details of the author, location and persistent identifier, and dispatch of a voucher copy and/or notification of the internet address of your digital offering" — e.g. https://www.e-rara.ch/zuz/content/titleinfo/14231134 . The specific title-info licence field for the Bibl 121 record was not rendered in the pages fetched in this pass, so the scan licence for this copy is recorded as **CANDIDATE (platform pattern: Public Domain Mark, free download with source indication requested) / NOT directly VERIFIED for this title**, while the underlying text's public-domain status is verified on the facts above.

**(d) House-use position.** Public domain text (1718/1719; all contributors d. by 1743); complete OCR'd digital text freely downloadable on e-rara — usable as citation/control; the scan's own per-title licence field should be read off the title-info page before bulk reuse of the scan files themselves.

## 5. "La Bibla u la Sontga Scartira dil Veder a Niev Testament", British and Foreign Bible Society, 1870 (Sursilvan)

**(a) Bibliographic identity.** *La bibla u la sontga scartira dil veder a niev testament: vertida en romonsch da la Ligia Grischa* — the whole Bible in the Romansh of the Grey League (Sursilvan), printed at Frankfurt am Main "sin ordinaziun ad a cust da la Societad Biblica Britannica ad externa" (at the order and cost of the British and Foreign Bible Society), 1869–70. Sources: Google Books bibliographic record — title as above, "Contributors: Lucius Gabriel, British and Foreign Bible Society; Publisher: Sin ordinaziun ad a cust da la Societad Biblica Britannica ad externa, 1870; Original from: University of Bern; Digitized: Sep 14, 2021" — https://books.google.com/books?id=4jN058CKfqQC&source=gbs_book_other_versions_r&cad=2 ; HathiTrust catalogue record 009784222 — "Published: Stampau à Francfort al Main, sin ordinaziun ad à cust da la Societad Biblica Britannica ad externa, 1869-70", language Raeto-Romance, physical description "551, 76, 277, 308 p.", viewability "Full view — University of Virginia" — https://catalog.hathitrust.org/Record/009784222 . The New Testament portion is Gabriel's translation (see §2(b)): https://books.google.com/books?id=sAMJAAAAQAAJ .

**(b) Digital text availability.** VERIFIED, in two places: (i) HathiTrust, listed as **Full view** (source copy: University of Virginia) — https://catalog.hathitrust.org/Record/009784222 ; (ii) Google Books, listed as **Full view – 1870** (source copy: University of Bern, digitised 14 September 2021) — https://books.google.com/books?id=4jN058CKfqQC&source=gbs_book_other_versions_r&cad=2 . The Online Books Page likewise lists it with "page images at HathiTrust" — https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/book/browse?type=lcsubc&key=Raeto%2dRomance%20languages%20%2d%2d%20Dialects&c=x .

**(c) Copyright / licence status.** Underlying text: **public domain — VERIFIED on two independent grounds.** (1) By age: published 1869–70; the translation rests on 17th-century work (Gabriel, d. 1663, per §2), and in any event a work published in 1870 is beyond any life-plus-70 term. (2) By the holding platform's own rights determination: HathiTrust's rights algorithm treats "books published outside the United States before 1901" as receiving "a 'public domain' rights status and become full view for everyone in the world to read" — https://cdlib.org/services/consortial-partnerships/hathitrust/hathitrust-rights-copyright-issues/ ; HathiTrust's own copyright page states the same approach — items are opened where they are "in the public domain" or permission supports the access given — https://www.hathitrust.org/the-collection/search-access/copyright-access/ . Google Books' own front matter in its public-domain scans states the principle that such a book "has survived long enough for the copyright to expire and the book to enter the public domain" — wording carried in Google-digitised public-domain volumes, e.g. https://ia601300.us.archive.org/17/items/alttestamentlic00gumpgoog/alttestamentlic00gumpgoog_djvu.txt (general Google Books public-domain notice, not this title's own scan).

**(d) House-use position.** Public domain text (1870); full-view digital text at HathiTrust and Google Books — usable as citation/control.

## 6. "Bibla ecumena romontscha" — modern ecumenical Bible (in progress)

**(a) Bibliographic identity.** An ecumenical Protestant–Catholic translation from the Hebrew, Aramaic and Greek into **Sursilvan** (not Rumantsch Grischun — the commission deliberately chose the Sursilvan idiom), planned in five volumes, work begun 1968. Volumes verified as published: New Testament **1988**; the Prophets **2004**; the Psalms **2010**; the Poetic Books **2014** (Psalms republished together with the Song of Songs); and the Pentateuch (the five books of Moses) in **2022**. The History Books remained outstanding at the latest reporting. Sources: the translation survey — "a plan to publish *Bibla ecumena romontscha* in five volumes. The New Testament was published in 1988, the Prophets of the Old Testament in 2004, and the Psalms in 2010. As of 2014, the Psalms have just been republished together with Songs. The Pentateuch and the History Books are forthcoming" — http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible_translations_into_Romansh ; the Catholic Church Switzerland profile of translator Martin Fontana — "1988: das Neue Testament. 2004: die prophetischen Bücher … 2010: die Psalmen. 2014: die poetischen Bücher" and "die ökumenische Übersetzung wurde in Auftrag gegeben … 1968 hat die Arbeit begonnen", the translation being into "Surselvische" (Sursilvan) — https://www.kath.ch/medienspiegel/das-schoenste-daran-eine-gemeinsame-bibel-die-beide-konfessionen-nutzen/ ; the 2022 Pentateuch volume — a Graubünden parish bulletin notes "Igl onn 2022 ei in niev tom dalla Bibla ecumena romontscha cumparius. El cuntegn ils tschun cudischs da Moses" ("In 2022 a new volume of the Bibla ecumena romontscha appeared; it contains the five books of Moses") — https://pfarrei-ilanz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Agenda_98-2023_Ilanz_Web.pdf . A Swiss Bible Society figure (its former president, Pastor Urs Jörg) is quoted in reporting on the project, and the desk brief names the Swiss Bible Society (Biel/Bienne) and the Geneva Bible Society as the relevant Bible-society links — http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible_translations_into_Romansh .

**(b) Digital text availability.** **None found.** No digital text of any *Bibla ecumena romontscha* volume was found on any platform checked in §7 or in general search in this pass; the work circulates in print.

**(c) Copyright / licence status.** **In copyright — VERIFIED as a position, on these facts:** the volumes are modern publications (1988, 2004, 2010, 2014, 2022) by a living translation project (work begun 1968 and still in progress at the latest reporting; a lead translator, Martin Fontana, received an honorary doctorate from the University of Zurich for this work in recent reporting — https://www.kath.ch/medienspiegel/das-schoenste-daran-eine-gemeinsame-bibel-die-beide-konfessionen-nutzen/ ). No open licence, public-domain statement or free-use term for the text was found on any page consulted. **Publisher / rights holder: CANDIDATE / NOT VERIFIED** — the sources consulted establish that the translation was ecumenically commissioned and is associated in reporting with the Swiss Bible Society's orbit, but no source fetched in this pass names the publisher or rights holder of the volumes in so many words, and this record does not guess one.

**(d) House-use position.** In copyright (1988–2022, project ongoing); no digital text found; publisher/rights holder not verified — fact and citation only, never a copying base.

## Closing answer — is there a complete historical Romansh Bible whose digital text is freely usable as a lexical control (citation-grade) for a modern Rumantsch Grischun build?

**Yes — two, on the evidence in this record.**

1. **The *Bibla da Cuera* (Sursilvan, Chur, 1718 [1719], the 1717–1719 edition)** — a complete Bible with Apocrypha. Its underlying text is public domain (published 1718/1719; every named contributor died by 1743 — §4(a), (c)). A complete digitised copy with a searchable OCR text layer stands on e-rara from the Zentralbibliothek Zürich copy (Bibl 121), freely downloadable under e-rara's stated terms (§4(b), (c)) — https://www.e-rara.ch/zuz/content/pagetext/9246688 . The one reservation, stated in the same breath: the per-title licence field of that specific e-rara record was not rendered in the fetched pages, so the scan licence is recorded as platform-pattern Public Domain Mark, CANDIDATE, while the text itself is verified public domain.
2. **The British and Foreign Bible Society Bible of 1870 (Sursilvan)** — a complete Bible, public domain by age and by HathiTrust's own rights determination for non-US works published before 1901, standing in **Full view** at both HathiTrust and Google Books (§5(b), (c)) — https://catalog.hathitrust.org/Record/009784222 and https://books.google.com/books?id=4jN058CKfqQC&source=gbs_book_other_versions_r&cad=2 .

Either text is therefore freely usable as a citation-grade lexical control. Both are Sursilvan-side historical texts; neither is Rumantsch Grischun (a 1982 standard), so their control value for an RG build is lexical and historical — attested Romansh forms — and the choice of control remains subject to the ruling the lane brief reserves (§4 of the brief: whether a 17th/18th-century idiom text is an acceptable control for a modern RG build, or whether Pledari Grond plus citation of the partial modern ecumenical text should carry the function). The earliest complete Bible, the Vallader Bible of 1679 (§3), is public domain as a text but has **no verified digital copy**, so it cannot yet serve as the digital control; Gabriel's 1648 Sursilvan New Testament (§2) is in the same position. The modern *Bibla ecumena romontscha* (§6) is in copyright, partial (History Books still outstanding), in Sursilvan rather than RG, and has no verified digital text — fact and citation only.
