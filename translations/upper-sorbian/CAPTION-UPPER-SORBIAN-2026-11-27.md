Friends, the Upper Sorbian Quran is on the shelf.

All 6,236 verses of the Quran, rendered into Upper Sorbian — hornjoserbšćina, the language of the Sorbs of Lusatia in eastern Germany — from my English Ayah Companion translation. 6,137 verses stand fully rendered. I am going to be plain with you about the rest, because plain is the only way I know how to do this: at 99 seats we could not find an attested Upper Sorbian word in any source we could reach — for the jinn, for hypocrites, for usury, for adultery, and a handful of others — and those seats are marked right in the text, in the open, where anybody can see them. We did not invent words and call them Sorbian. That is not how this work is done.

Every other word in this book traces to the published record — the dictionaries, the public-domain Sorbian Bibles, and the sources named in the glossary that travels with the book, so any checker can walk any word back to where it was found. Where a rendering rests on a construction instead of an attested word, the glossary and the disclosure file say so. The book failed its independent checker twice on spelling discipline before it passed — both failures and every repair are on the record in the lane's papers, because a book that hides its repairs has something to hide.

This translation was prepared with the help of artificial intelligence, under my direction. I am a translator in Hays, Kansas, not a speaker of Upper Sorbian, and the book says that about itself. It is not for sale. It is a gift — free to read, free to download, free to pass on. That is the whole point of the Ayah Companion: the most people having the most access.
