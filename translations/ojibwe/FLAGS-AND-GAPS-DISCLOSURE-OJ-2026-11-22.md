## Variations carried as disclosed variation, not unified

- **Prostration notes: 15 notes in 12 distinct phrasings.** The
  manuscript carries 16 note lines in all — the 5:6 translator's
  note plus 15 prostration notes in 12 distinct Ojibwe phrasings
  (the checker's exact count; for example *[aapiji mino-ayaa:
  zhiiwaande]* ×3, *[mino-ayaa apiji ayami'idiwag]* ×2, and 10
  singletons including *[ayaamoyaa — mino-doodam]* and
  *[ayaamoyaa — maajii-doodam]*). The brief requires one phrasing
  for "recommended" and one for "obligatory"; unifying them at
  the desk would require assigning each seat a type and a
  canonical wording without speaker review, so the variation is
  carried as disclosed variation, not unified.
- **Conjunct ending variation (-oowaad / -aawaad).** Plural
  conjunct endings vary between -oowaad and -aawaad in a handful
  of high-frequency verbs (checker counts: debwe'endamoowaad 39
  vs debwe'endamaawaad 4; maajaawaad 34, with maajiiwaad 6;
  gikendamoowaad 30 vs gikendamaawaad 3; -oowaad forms 557
  substring hits in all). The variation is real and undisguised;
  it was not silently unified, because choosing canonical Ojibwe
  morphology at the desk, without a speaker, would be invention.

## The variety caveat

The Bible control text is the northwestern Ontario (Saulteaux)
variety of Ojibwe, while The Ojibwe People's Dictionary
(University of Minnesota, about 30,000 entries) is Minnesota
Ojibwe. Both are double-vowel, and the draft draws on both pillars
— the dictionary for fact only, never its wording or arrangement.
Term choices may therefore carry either variety's colouring.
