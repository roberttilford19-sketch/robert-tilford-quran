## Orthography and base (FIXED)
- Norm: Mooring (Bökingharde Frisian, endonym Frasch), the North
  Frisian dialect of the Risum-Lindholm / Niebüll area, in its
  modern spelling as attested in English Wiktionary's Mooring-
  tagged entries and the LearnFrisian Mooring wordlist (itself
  checked against friesisch.net and Wiktionary). North Frisian
  ONLY, ISO 639-3 frr, Mooring dialect only.
- DIALECT BAR: a form seats only if it is attested FOR MOORING —
  (a) a sense of its Wiktionary entry is tagged Mooring and the
  seated form is the headword (where no Mooring alternative form
  exists) or a form tagged Mooring, or (b) its entry carries no
  dialect tag on any sense (pan-dialect entry, e.g. the pronoun
  hi), or (c) it stands in the LearnFrisian Mooring column.
  Forms attested only for Söl'ring, Fering-Öömrang, Heligoland,
  Halligen, Wiedingharde or Goesharde are NEVER seated.
- SATERLAND / WEST FRISIAN BAR: Saterland Frisian (stq, parked
  UNCERTAIN) is never touched and never seated; West Frisian
  (fry; the desk's `frisian` lane, PARKED as DRAFT — NOT
  PUBLISHED) is never touched and never seated. LearnFrisian's
  West Frisian column was used only as the page's meaning key,
  never as a source of forms.
- HISTORICAL CONTROL BAR: Bendsen (1860), *Die nordfriesische
  Sprache nach der Moringer Mundart* (public domain), was held
  as historical corroboration only. Its 1860 spellings (e.g.
  Godd / Göd for God, Senne for sin) were NOT seated as modern
  Mooring forms, exactly as the Võro lane barred 1686 forms.
  Consequence, disclosed on the book's face: God, lord, heaven,
  angel, soul, spirit, mercy and sin have no modern Mooring seat
  in the held inventories and stand as GAPs throughout —
  including in the Basmalah. The brief predicted this cost for a
  non-Söl'ring dialect (no same-dialect Bible control).

## Names policy
Personal names and scripture titles are carried in the Master's
forms (Moses, Jesus, Mary, Abraham, Noah, the Torah, the Gospel,
the Psalms, the Quran…), unaltered. The disjointed letters at
surah heads are carried as in the Master ("Alif, Lam, Meem.").

## The Basmalah
Surah 1:1 IS the Basmalah and is translated as verse 1:1. Every
other surah except 9 carries it as an unnumbered opening line.
Seated form (fixed for the whole book, exactly as the engine
renders 1:1 under the gate law):
**Önj noome ⟦GAP: god⟧, ⟦GAP: beneficent⟧, ⟦GAP: merciful⟧.**
("In the name of God, the Beneficent, the Merciful" — önj "in"
and noome "name" are attested Mooring; God and both mercy names
have no modern Mooring seat in the held inventories and stand
as GAPs, disclosed here and on the book's face rather than
filled from Bendsen's 1860 spelling or another dialect. Verse
1:3 is the same two mercy names without the frame:
"⟦GAP: beneficent⟧, ⟦GAP: merciful⟧.")

## GAP policy
A seat that cannot be carried on attested Mooring forms takes
the visible marker ⟦GAP: <english term>⟧ inline, the rest of
the verse rendered as far as attested forms allow. NEVER invent
a form; NEVER borrow silently from another North Frisian
dialect, Saterland Frisian, West Frisian, German, Dutch, Danish
or English.

## Grammar reference (the norm every batch follows)
Mooring is a West Germanic language with grammatical gender,
inflected verbs and V2 main-clause order. This first-pass draft
follows a disclosed plain style, kept close to the Master,
clause by clause:
- Pronouns: ik (I), dü (you sg; seated for all "you" by the
  plain device), hi (he), jü (she), hat (it), we (we), jam (you
  pl), ja (they / them); object forms me, de, ham, har, üs, ja;
  possessives man (my), dan (your sg), san (his / its), harn
  (her), üüsen (our), jarnge (your pl), jare (their).
- Articles (the / a / an) and the genitive linker "of" seat as
  zero — a disclosed plain-style device: Mooring articles are
  gendered (di / jü / dåt / da) and gender cannot be assigned
  mechanically per noun without risking wrong gender
  throughout; the independent checker is asked to resolve this.
- Nouns seat in the nominative dictionary form; the plural
  seats wherever the Master has a plural, by the attested
  plural form held in sources/mooring-plurals.json where one is
  held for that entry, otherwise by the same dictionary form
  (disclosed fallback).
- Verbs seat in the dictionary (infinitive) form — a disclosed
  plain-style device: person and tense are carried by the
  subject and by time words, exactly as the sibling lanes
  disclose their plain-style devices.
- Negation: **ai** + verb (the attested Mooring negative).
- "Say:" introducing speech — Seede: with the Master's
  quotation marks (“ ”) kept.
- Numbers as in the Master (words where the Master has words):
  ån (1), twäär (2), tra (3), fjouer (4), fiiw (5), seeks (6),
  soowen (7), oocht (8), nüügen (9), tiin (10), hunert (100).
  All are Mooring-attested inventory forms. "Thousand" has no
  held Mooring seat and stands as a GAP.
- Every seated lemma MUST trace to the harvested Mooring
  inventory (sources/mooring-forms.json) or to a disclosed
  regular derivation of one (the attested-plural device above);
  an untraced word does not seat — it takes the GAP marker.
