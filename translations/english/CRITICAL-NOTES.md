# Critical Notes

## Surah 3:7 — the fitnah reading (critical note, entered 2026-10-25)

The Arabic phrase at issue is ابْتِغَاءَ الْفِتْنَةِ (*ibtighā' al-fitnah*). Elsewhere in the Quran, *fitnah* carries the senses of trial, persecution, and discord (cf. 2:191).

The Locked English Master reads “seeking descent.” The full clause is: “they follow the unspecific part, seeking descent, and seeking to derive an interpretation.”

The daughter translations preserve contrary evidence. The majority render the discord sense: Afrikaans has *tweedrag*; Chinese has 纷争, meaning strife; Alemannic has *Zwietracht*; and Persian reads *fitnah* in the discord sense. A minority carried the master's word across: Bavarian has *Abstieg*, and Bengali uses its descent word.

In the fleet sense-sample, 33 of 34 seated verdicts were SAME SENSE, and this verse was the standing flag. The Chinese lane's process note also flags the same construction at Surah 17:7.

**Ruling — Robert Tilford, 2026-10-25:** The master's reading **stands** as the editor's deliberate choice. This note preserves the contrary evidence openly, and the question remains challengeable on the record. The similar construction at Surah 17:7 is flagged as the same construction, but it is **not ruled** by this decision; it awaits its own decision.

## Surah 17:7 — the ʿalaw clause (critical note, entered 2026-10-25)

The Arabic final clause at issue is وَلِيُتَبِّرُوا مَا عَلَوْا تَتْبِيرًا (*wa-li-yutabbirū mā ʿalaw tabbīran*): مَا is the relative pronoun "what"; عَلَوْا is a perfect verb, "they had risen over / had mastered"; تَتْبِيرًا is the verbal noun of intensity, "with utter destruction." The perfect aspect denotes what was already mastered, not whatever may later fall into a hand.

The Locked English Master reads, in full: "If you work righteousness, you work righteousness for yourselves; and if you commit evil, you do so against yourselves. Then, when the second promise comes true, they will make your faces filled with sorrow, and enter the Temple as they entered it the first time, and utterly destroy all that falls into their power." The final clause — "all that falls into their power" — carries a prospective, open shape where the Arabic's is completed.

The Chinese daughter preserves the completed sense. Its clause reads 彻底毁灭他们所掌控的一切 — "utterly destroy everything they controlled." The divergence was found by the Chinese lane's own process note under blind audit: at this entry the English, not the Chinese, departs from the Arabic, and it was logged for the editor's English-side ruling. A relayed external audit (Grok Expert, 2026-10-25) was graded at the desk against the Arabic and the house texts; its grammar finding concurs with the above, and its citations of published translations (Ma Jian and others) were not relied on, standing unverified in the grade file.

**Ruling — Robert Tilford, 2026-10-25:** The master's reading **stands** as the editor's deliberate choice, as at Surah 3:7. This note preserves the contrary evidence openly — the Arabic's completed aspect and the daughter's rendering that tracks it — and the question remains challengeable on the record.
