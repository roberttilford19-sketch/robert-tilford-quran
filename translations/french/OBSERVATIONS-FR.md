## 2. Terminology decisions for the native reviewer

- **God = Dieu throughout; body "Allah" count = 0** (verified). Per the brief, this is flagged for the reviewer: many French readers may expect "Allah". "Mahomet" was not introduced; "Muhammad" is kept as in the English (4 verse-body occurrences; surah 47 title also Muhammad).
- **"The Reminder" = "le Rappel"** fixed by the brief (5 occurrences counted in verse body), applied at 41:41 and 43:5 — the Urdu two-renderings weak spot did not recur.
- **Divine epithets beyond the brief's fixed pair vary where the English varies:** the Beneficent = "le Bienfaisant", the Merciful = "le Miséricordieux", but "Most Merciful" appears mainly as "Très Miséricordieux" (25×), with "le plus Miséricordieux" (2×) and "le Compatissant" (1× at 55:1 by flag context). A French reviewer should decide whether to collapse these, and whether Rahman should instead follow the familiar French convention "le Tout Miséricordieux".
- **jinn** is not fully normed across slices: "djinns" 19×, "djinn" 3×, "jinns" 2×, "jinn" 1×. Needs one project-wide ruling (ordinary French favours djinn/djinns).
- **People-name forms are mixed by necessity:** established French forms used where obvious (Noé, Moïse, Salomon, Jésus, Marie; Ézéchiel/Énoch/Ismaël at 21:85), while "Thamood" (8×), "Hud" (7×), "Shuaib" (11×) and "Saleh" (9×) remain in or near the English spellings in several slices. A norming pass should decide (e.g. Houd/Chouaïb/Salih only if the reviewer confirms them as the established forms to use consistently).
- **zakat/almsgiving:** "aumône obligatoire" for the obligatory charity and "aumône"/"charité" elsewhere — **no percentage anywhere** (verified 0 "%" signs in body and file), per the Quran-alone rule.
- Fixed glossary otherwise held: Seigneur, Messager/prophète, le Livre, le Coran, l'Écriture, l'Au-delà, le Jardin, le Paradis, le Feu/l'Enfer, prière ("accomplir la prière"), jeûne, pèlerinage/hajj, croyants/mécréants, injustes, hypocrites, signes, Jour du Jugement / Jour de la Résurrection. Singular address is tu (including to God); plural audience is vous; quotations use « … ».

## 3. Literal renderings — known weak spots for a native reader

- **81:4** — "When the relationships are suspended" rendered literally: "Quand les relations seront suspendues" (flagged; reviewer may want an idiomatic alternative).
- **83:9, 83:20** — "A numerical book" rendered literally "Un livre numéroté" (likely "un registre écrit").
- **Kept-literal items for review:** 41:10 "stabilisateurs" (likely mountains), 68:42 "la Jambe" capitalized as a body term, and dense legal/household verses that mirror the English's heavy single sentences (e.g. 4:11–12, 33:50–53).
- **Transliterations kept as in the English** pending a norming pass: Sijjeen, Elliyyeen/Elliyyoon (source spelling itself varies, flagged at 83:18), Salsabeel, at-Tariq (86:1), Quraish; idol names and rare place names should be checked by the reviewer rather than silently normalized.
- Register variance across the 12 translators is expected. Sensitive passages (4:34, 24:2–9, 24:31–33) were translated plainly and literally — a French reviewer should check tone and legal naturalness.

## 4. Structure and discipline

- Format identical to the Portuguese/Italian builds: French title block (MANUSCRIT FRANÇAIS — ÉBAUCHE DE RÉVISION, pas une édition définitive; not-a-first line acknowledging the existing French tradition, including Muhammad Hamidullah), Sommaire, Partie I · La Traduction, 114 sections with the Arabic surah name, an italic intro line, basmalah Arabic + italic fixed-formula line "Au nom de Dieu, le Bienfaisant, le Miséricordieux." in the 112 surahs that carry the frame (surah 1 counts it as 1:1; surah 9 has none), the translator's note inside 5:6 with its continuation after it ("Certaines lectures prolongent l’essuyage jusqu’aux pieds ; la lecture imprimée ici dit laver."), and 15 prostration markers (11 *[prosternation — recommandée]*, 4 *[prosternation — obligatoire]*) after the verse's Arabic line.
- Quran-alone discipline: no hadith/tafsir material imported, no traditional specifics added, no percentage for zakat.
- The audit is **structural** (counts, sequences, the Arabic character-for-character, markers, residue scan), as for the other drafts of this project: it is not a verse-by-verse philological proof. Final language quality requires a native French reviewer.
