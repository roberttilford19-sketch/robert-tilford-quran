The Arabic is *at-tannur* (the oven/furnace); the English master says "volcano," and the English is the fidelity target.

**11:64** — "Gott sei kameel-fraw" for "the she-camel of God" — descriptive compound on attested *kameel*; no single attested word for a she-camel.

**13 (title)** — "Da Dooner" for "The Thunder" — *dooner* is attested (Lins 1895; attested lexicon), while the Bible control shows the verb form *dunnaht*; the noun form follows the dictionary.

**16 (title), 16:68** — "di imme / Imme" for the bee — *imme* stands in the attested lexicon, but the Bible control has no bee passage to confirm the singular in use; the surah intro's "hoonich-shtokk" (honeycomb/hive) is a descriptive compound on attested *hoonich*.

**11:50 ff.** — Names *Saleh* and *Shuaib* follow the English master chunk's spelling (the chunk prints "Saleh," "Shuaib"); the brief's example list gives the transliterations "Salih, Shu'ayb." The chunk was followed as fidelity target. *Thamood, Median, Judi, Aad* likewise follow the chunk.

**12:30** — "em goffahnah sei fraw" for "the governor's wife" — *goffahnah* is a phonetic loan spelling for governor (Arabic *al-aziz*, the mighty one); no attested Bible word covers this office. Also at 12:51, 12:78, 12:88.

**15:87** — "sivva funn di poahra" for "seven of the pairs" (al-mathani) — rendered literally; the phrase's exact reference is uncertain in the English itself.

**16:67** — "zukkah" for the English "sugar" (from date-palms and grapevines) — the English master was followed; the Arabic *sakar* is often read as an intoxicating drink, but no traditional gloss was imported, per Quran-alone.

**14:5** — "di Dawga funn Gott" for "the Days of God" — rendered literally as the English has it; the phrase is a descriptive rendering, not a Bible idiom.

**General** — A number of ordinary words were spelled on the Bible's phonemic pattern where the control text's exact token could not be grepped in the time available (e.g. *voohnah, frayt, shats, karavahn, bechah, gfangnis*). All are built from attested roots/patterns, but a full attestation sweep of every token against the lexicon remains a checker-lane task.
