# Flags Adjudication — Lakota Lane, 2026-11-22

A dated layer over FLAGS-LKT.md, written under the desk/source resolution frame of Robert's ruling of 2026-11-18. FLAGS-LKT.md itself is not edited. The per-bullet record stands in `wave/flags-adjudication.json`, one verdict and one line of grounds for each of the register's 1,371 bullets.

## Method

The adjudication rests on the wave's final grounding record. The manuscript's 7,011 distinct forms divide without overlap into 1,688 forms carrying a published-citation receipt — 1,240 new receipts in `wave/receipts-wave-final.json`, 314 receipts from the glossary lane in `concordance-full-lkt.json`, and 134 published receipts of 2026-11-18 — and 5,323 forms listed in `wave/unreceipted-final.json` that were examined and stand unattested in the published record. The classification frame is the clause triage of 2026-11-18, which sorted 2,505 clauses across the same 1,371 bullets into lexical attestation questions (kind A, 1,122 clauses), grammar and morphology questions (kind B, 106), semantic adequacy, nuance and idiom (kind C, 272), term choice among variants (kind D, 11), orthography (kind E, 95), residual rendering notes (kind F, 864), and fidelity to the English and Arabic master (kind G, 35).

For each bullet, the forms it questions were taken from the register's `forms_mentioned` entries that are concordance forms, including the individual forms inside the multiword phrases the register lists; where the register lists no form, Lakota tokens in the bullet's own text that resolve to a concordance form were used, with ordinary English words set aside. Each form was then read against the receipt sets and against the reasons recorded in `wave/notreceipted-band-*.json` and `wave/notreceipted-sweep.json` for the forms that were withheld. The bullet's own wording decided the open clauses: where a bullet mixes a settled attestation question with a semantic or grammar question, the most open clause governs the verdict. A settled-attestation verdict settles only that a checkable published source attests the form and its gloss; it does not settle, and this layer nowhere claims, that the rendering in any verse is right.

## The verdict counts

Of the 1,371 bullets, 14 are settled on attestation: every Lakota form the bullet questions now carries a published-citation receipt, so the existence and meaning question the bullet raised is answered by a checkable source. Forty bullets are confirmed the other way: the form they question was examined and stands unattested, so the flag's suspicion is confirmed by the record and the form stands as a disclosed gap. Three hundred and twenty-one bullets are partly settled — some questioned forms grounded and some not, or an attestation question answered while a grammar or semantic question in the same bullet remains open. The remaining bullets are open or carry no question at all: 858 raise semantic questions of adequacy, nuance, idiom or term choice that no dictionary lookup settles; 40 raise grammar or morphology pattern questions; 34 raise fidelity-to-master questions that a desk comparison could settle but this wave did not perform; and 64 mention no concordance form and pose no checkable question, most of them transliterations kept under the locked-name rule or pure notes such as surah headers.

In terms of the forms involved, 1,259 of the 1,371 bullets resolve to at least one concordance form. Across all bullets, 1,304 distinct concordance forms are involved in 4,367 form mentions, of which 3,050 mentions are of receipted forms and 1,317 are of ungrounded forms. The 249 bullets that carry the register's unverified marker divide as follows: 185 partly settled, 33 open on grammar, 11 settled on attestation, 9 confirmed ungrounded, 6 open on semantics, and 5 open on fidelity.

## What is now attested

The forms the flags returned to most often are, in the main, now grounded. The negative particle šni, questioned in 151 bullet mentions, the definite article kiŋ in 123, the future marker kta in 84, táku in 93, wašté in 71, na in 53, wičháša in 51, tȟáŋka in 50, uŋ in 33, yuhápi in 31, čhaŋkú in 23, and wóiyukčaŋ in 25 all carry published-citation receipts. For bullets that questioned only these forms' existence or gloss, the attestation question is closed, and the per-bullet record names the receipts' grounding in each case. Where those same bullets also complained of a paraphrase, a flattened nuance, or a loose gloss, that complaint survives the receipt, and the bullet is recorded as partly settled or open on semantics accordingly — a receipt attests a word, not a rendering.

On grammar, the published grammars named in the receipts do speak to several of the patterns the flags questioned, and the affected bullets are recorded as partly settled with the citation named in their grounds. Riggs's 1893 Dakota Grammar supplies the pronoun and possessive paradigms at sections 15–16. The Ullrich Grammar Handbook, cited through its published table of contents, covers the yaŋke and yaŋkapi sitting paradigm, the comitative kičhí, the result conjunction čha, and a unit on "but." The grammar material carried on Wikipedia's Lakota language page, sourced there to Rood and Taylor 1996 and to the New Lakota Dictionary, covers the plural suffix -pi, the sentence-final e-ablaut, and the wó- nominalisation. These sources settle that the patterns exist and how they are formed; whether the draft applies them correctly at any given seat remains a desk judgement, which is why no grammar bullet is recorded as fully settled.

## What stands ungrounded

The largest disclosed gaps are forms the manuscript uses hundreds of times. The ečháŋ family stands unattested as a family: ečháŋ itself, at 306 occurrences and questioned in 15 bullet mentions, and ečháŋl, at 618 occurrences — the manuscript's most frequent ungrounded form — were both carried through the wide-web sweep and found in none of the Comparative Siouan Dictionary's Lakota or Dakota rows, the Kaikki and Wiktionary Lakota entries, Ingham's dictionary, the UCLA word list, or the published Lakota prose searched; the sweep records that the plural shape ečháŋpi cannot be receipted while the base form itself is unattested, and that the final -l of ečháŋl is explained by no published rule found. The conjunction čhaŋna, at 444 occurrences and questioned in 22 bullet mentions, stands the same way: absent from every staged and swept source. The comparative iyéčhel, at 312 occurrences and questioned in 13 bullet mentions, is a narrower case — the sweep found the demonstrative adverb paradigm attested for héčhel and léčhel, but iyéčhel itself appears in none of the published sources checked, so the form stands ungrounded on the record as it stands. The adversative Tká and tká, at 250 and 131 occurrences and touched by the flags in a single bullet's recommended prostration note, present a spelling question rather than an existence question: published Lakota attests tkȟá, "but, however," including in a 1944 Clark and Afraid of Hawk text and in the Grammar Handbook's unit 61, but the manuscript's h-less spelling tká is not attested in the sources checked. The comitative pair om and ob, at 145 and 86 occurrences and questioned in one and eight bullet mentions respectively, were withheld on ambiguity and the withholding stands: the staged sources offered only reverse-dictionary noise, no checkable attestation of either use was found, and the m-final shape of om is unexplained against the ob and ób shapes. To these the flags add wóie, the draft's count noun for "verses" at 85 occurrences and 22 bullet mentions, which likewise stands unattested.

## What remains open, and why

Most of the register remains open, and it is open for a reason no further receipt pass can remove. The 858 semantic bullets ask whether a rendering carries the source's meaning — whether a paraphrase flattens a legal formula, whether a loose gloss drops a nuance, whether a term chosen among attested variants sits in the right register. Published dictionaries attest forms and glosses; they do not grade renderings, so these bullets stand as desk judgement questions, to be worked seat by seat against the master text. The 40 grammar bullets ask whether a pattern — an imperative ending, a causative, a conjugation, a word order — is correctly formed at a given seat; the published grammars named above speak to the patterns in general, and the residue is application, which is likewise desk work. The 34 fidelity bullets, most of them carrying the register's own FIDELITY label, name places where the rendering may have shifted, compressed or omitted something in the English or Arabic master; a direct desk comparison of manuscript against master would settle them, and this wave, which was a grounding wave, did not perform that comparison. The 40 confirmed-ungrounded bullets and the ungrounded share of the 321 partly settled bullets are closed as questions of evidence: the forms stand as disclosed gaps in the manuscript, named here and in `wave/unreceipted-final.json`, until and unless a published source attesting them is found.

Nothing in this layer is described as awaiting any outside review. Under the governing frame, what remains is desk work against published sources and the master text, and the adjudication records exactly how far the published record, as it stands on 2026-11-22, can take each flag.
