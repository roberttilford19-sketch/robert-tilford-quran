# Lakota Completion Wave — Report, 2026-11-22

*Commissioned by Robert Tilford on 2026-11-22: a whole wave reserved to finish the Lakota lane — the grounding and concordance work the shelf record honestly carried as undone. Fourteen workers ran under one coordinator: a lexicon builder, an inventory registrar, eight grounding workers on frequency bands, a second-round sweep, a flags adjudicator, a concordance rebuilder, and an independent checker who had done none of the grounding. The Quiet Protocol stood throughout — no outreach of any kind was made. The manuscript's verse text was not edited by the wave; its MD5 is d5467e986832cc110b7dcbbae38024af, byte-identical before and after.*

## What was unfinished when the wave went out

The lane's own records stated the position plainly, and the wave's inventory verified every figure by re-extracting the manuscript and reconciling it against the concordance: the manuscript holds 6,236 verses in 114 surahs, 114,365 word tokens, and 7,011 distinct word forms, and the full concordance covers every one of them — zero forms missing, zero extra, zero count mismatches. Of those 7,011 forms, 448 carried a receipt of some kind: 314 glossary-derived lane receipts (locked terms, proper names, surah titles, key terms and their recorded variants) carrying no published citation, and 134 published-citation receipts from the receipt pass of 2026-11-18. That left 6,563 forms carrying 38,205 occurrences with no receipt at all. The New Lakota Dictionary Online, the one openly readable lemmatising dictionary that could have carried the long tail, was tested live on 2026-11-22 and remains out of service, as it has been since mid-2024; no attempt was made to work around that, and no receipt in this wave claims to have consulted it. The flags file held 1,371 bullets (2,505 clauses by the 2026-11-18 triage), of which roughly half posed questions a dictionary can settle and half posed questions only judgement can.

## What the wave did

The lexicon builder first assembled every openly published source that could be machine-read: Wiktionary's Lakota entries as extracted by Kaikki.org (890 entries), the Comparative Siouan Dictionary's 1,916 Lakota forms with their Buechel and Riggs page citations, and the full texts of Riggs's Dakota-English Dictionary of 1892 and Williamson's English-Dakota Dictionary of 1902, both public domain and openly readable on the Internet Archive — 22,490 normalised headword keys in all. The eight grounding workers then took the 6,563 unreceipted forms in frequency order, from the forms occurring twenty times or more down to the 2,975 forms that occur exactly once, and a ninth worker swept the residue a second time against the enlarged root set and against the wider published web — Ingham's English-Lakota Dictionary, Ella Deloria's glossed texts, published Lakota teaching materials and scholarship. One standard governed every receipt, and it was Robert's dictionary fair-use law: take the fact only — the word and its meaning — state every gloss in the desk's own words, and cite the source so any checker can walk the word back. A form standing in a published Lakota source earned a Basis A receipt; a regular derivation of such a form — a plural in -pi, a sentence-final ablaut form, a possessive or person-prefix paradigm form, a wó- nominalisation, a capital or spelling variant — earned Basis B with the rule named. The two Dakota dictionaries, being a sister dialect and not Lakota itself, could support at most Basis B, with the correspondence stated in the receipt. Anything whose attestation was weak, ambiguous, or at odds with the manuscript's use was refused, and the refusal and its reason were recorded. No Lakota form was invented at any point in the wave.

## The counted result

The wave wrote 1,240 new published-citation receipts — 232 on Basis A and 1,008 on Basis B — covering 12,485 occurrences. With the earlier layers, 1,688 of the manuscript's 7,011 distinct forms now carry a receipt (24.1 percent of forms), and those receipts cover 88,645 of its 114,365 occurrences (77.5 percent of the text as read). Of the receipts, 1,374 forms carry a published citation a checker can follow — 298 on Basis A and 1,076 on Basis B across the 2026-11-18 pass and this wave — and the 314 glossary lane receipts stand as they were, honestly labelled as carrying no published citation of their own. Every band's two output files were reconciled by machine against its worklist: no form receipted twice, no form lost between receipted and not-receipted, no occurrence count altered. The rebuilt concordance in the landing pack was likewise computed from its merged entries, and the independent checker recomputed every headline number from that file and found zero disagreement.

What remains ungrounded is 5,323 forms carrying 25,720 occurrences (22.5 percent of the text). Every one of those forms was individually examined, most of them twice, and every one stands in the pack's gaps register with the reason recorded — no attestation found, attestation ambiguous, attestation at odds with the manuscript's use, a proper name or Arabic transliteration, or a suspected one-off coinage of the draft's workers. The largest blocks deserve naming in the open, because they are the draft's real wounds. The ečháŋ family — ečháŋl at 618 occurrences, ečháŋ at 306, ečháŋpi at 127, over a thousand occurrences in all — was searched across every source the wave could reach, including the wider web, and stands nowhere in the published record; the final -l of ečháŋl is explained by no published rule the wave could find. Čhaŋna (444 occurrences), the h-less spellings Tká and tká (381 together, where published Lakota writes the conjunction tkȟá), iyéčhel (312, whose paradigm neighbours héčhel and ečhél the sweep did attest), the om / ob / ób group (298 together, withheld on ambiguity), and wóie (85) stand the same way. Two English words, "grape" and "date," were also found standing untranslated in the text. These forms have not been removed or replaced: they stand in the manuscript as they were written, and they stand in the concordance and the gaps register as examined-and-unattested — disclosed gaps, not hidden ones. Replacing the great coinages with attested alternatives would be a translation decision, and the wave was not commissioned to re-translate; it is named here as the largest single piece of work that remains.

## The flags

All 1,371 flag bullets were adjudicated against the finished grounding record, in a dated layer (FLAGS-ADJUDICATION-2026-11-22.md) that leaves FLAGS-LKT.md itself untouched. Fourteen bullets are settled on attestation — every form they questioned now carries a published receipt. Three hundred twenty-one are partly settled: their attestation questions are answered and the published grammars speak to their patterns, while a judgement the desk cannot take from a dictionary rides in the same bullet. Forty bullets are confirmed the other way — the form they questioned stands ungrounded, and the flag's suspicion is now a matter of record. The remainder stay open, and the report does not dress them up: 858 bullets turn on semantic adequacy — whether a flattened or paraphrased rendering still carries its verse's meaning — 40 on grammar in application, and 34 on fidelity to the English and Arabic masters, a comparison this grounding wave did not perform. Sixty-four bullets name no checkable form at all. Nothing in the adjudication is framed as awaiting native review; under Robert's ruling of 2026-11-18 the desk's verification is the standard, and what the desk cannot settle from published sources is recorded as open, with the reason.

## The independent check

The checker, who had done none of the grounding, ran six checks and passed the lane on all six. The counts recompute exactly. The manuscript's integrity is confirmed by hash on both copies. Every one of the 1,374 published receipts carries at least one source citation, every one of the 5,323 unreceipted forms stands in the gaps register with its reason, and no form is both receipted and registered as a gap. A seeded random sample of forty receipts — seventeen Basis A, twenty-three Basis B, seventeen resting on the Dakota dictionaries — was verified one by one against the cited sources themselves: forty passed, none failed, none proved unverifiable, and every sampled derivation mechanically produces the form it claims from the root it cites. The glosses were checked for copying and are the desk's own words; the only near-matches are single-concept glosses ("where?", "a young man") where no other honest wording exists. The checker named one defect, a citation inaccuracy rather than a grounding failure: the receipt for Wówašté described its 2026-11-18 root as Basis A where that root is Basis B. The form, its meaning, and its derivation all verify, and the citation has been corrected in the wave's records and in the merged concordance, with the counts unchanged.

## What remains, and the landing

Three things remain, stated plainly. First, the 5,323 unattested forms stand in the text, the largest of them woven through the draft's connective tissue; the grounding question is closed — the openly published record has been searched to its limit, and the New Lakota Dictionary's return, or a lemmatised Buechel in openly readable form, is the only road that could move them — but the translation question they pose is open, and it is Robert's. Second, the 932 flag bullets that remain open on semantic, grammar, and fidelity grounds are desk judgements still to be worked, none of them hidden. Third, the 314 glossary lane receipts still rest on the lane's own records rather than published citations.

The landing pack stands assembled in the wave's folder: the manuscript byte-identical, the glossary unchanged, the rebuilt concordance carrying the finished counts and an honest status — every form the openly published record attests now carries its citation, and every form it does not attest is listed as a disclosed gap — the gaps register, and this report's companion records. The loop the commission specified is complete: inventory verified, grounding carried to the limit of the published record, flags adjudicated as far as sources allow, concordance rebuilt and reconciled by count, and an independent check passed on every axis. On that basis the coordinator's assessment is that the lane is ready to land under the Pipeline Landing Authorization, as a completed, desk-verified draft whose gaps and open judgements are disclosed in the pack itself rather than smoothed over — and the desk settles the landing.
